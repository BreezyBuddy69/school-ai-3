## Why short stories?

Short stories sind im Englischunterricht der Unterstufe ein klassisches Sprungbrett zur längeren Romananalyse (siehe Jahr 5, z. B. "The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time"): Sie sind kurz genug, um vollständig gelesen zu werden, enthalten aber alle klassischen Analysekategorien.

## Structure of a short story

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Exposition** | Setting, characters and situation are introduced |
| **Rising action** | events build up tension/conflict |
| **Climax** | the turning point / most intense moment |
| **Falling action** | consequences of the climax unfold |
| **Resolution/Ending** | conflict is resolved — often with a **twist** or an open ending |

> Merke: Viele klassische englische Kurzgeschichten enden mit einem **"twist ending"** (unerwartete Wendung) — typisches Beispiel dafür sind die Kurzgeschichten von **Roald Dahl**, der neben Kinderbüchern auch für Erwachsene geschriebene, oft schwarzhumorige Short Stories mit überraschenden Enden verfasst hat (z. B. "Lamb to the Slaughter", "The Landlady").

## Key analytical categories

### 1. Plot
What happens? Summarise briefly in chronological order (or note if the story uses **flashback**/non-linear order).

### 2. Characters

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Protagonist** | the main character |
| **Antagonist** | the character/force opposing the protagonist |
| **Round character** | complex, multi-dimensional, can surprise the reader |
| **Flat character** | one-dimensional, defined by a single trait |
| **Dynamic character** | changes over the course of the story |
| **Static character** | stays the same |

### 3. Point of view (Narrative perspective)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **First-person narrator** | "I" — tells only what they themselves know/feel |
| **Third-person limited** | narrator focuses on one character's thoughts |
| **Third-person omniscient** | narrator knows everything about all characters |
| **Unreliable narrator** | the narrator's account cannot be fully trusted |

### 4. Setting

Time and place of the story — often shapes atmosphere and even plot (e.g. an isolated house, a specific historical period).

### 5. Theme

The central idea/message — often not stated directly but has to be inferred from plot, characters and symbols.

### 6. Literary devices

| Device | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Foreshadowing** | hints at events to come | a character mentions a storm before a disaster happens |
| **Symbolism** | an object/person represents something beyond itself | a broken clock symbolising a frozen moment in time |
| **Irony** | contrast between expectation and reality | a fire station burns down |
| **Suspense** | tension that keeps the reader engaged | withholding information about the outcome |
| **Twist ending** | an unexpected turn revealed at the end | the "culprit" turns out to be someone unexpected |

> Achtung Prüfung: Bei der Analyse muss jede genannte Kategorie (device, character type, point of view) immer mit einem **konkreten Textbeleg** verknüpft werden — reine Begriffsnennung ohne Beispiel aus dem Text gilt nicht als vollständige Analyse.

## Writing a short story analysis — structure

1. **Introduction:** title, author, brief summary of the situation
2. **Body:** plot development, characters, point of view, setting, key devices — each with textual evidence
3. **Conclusion:** overall theme/message, personal response (if asked for)
